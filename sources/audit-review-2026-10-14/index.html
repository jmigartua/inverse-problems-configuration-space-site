Method

Audit log (L009) · Audit publication (L010) · This review's log (L012) · The audit · Sources

The short version: the audit is good, and it is accepted almost whole. Its seven priority corrections are all right, and the three about the Daun paper (C33, C34, C35) were confirmed today against the printed pages: one sentence of the Drude case reverses what the authors say about surface scattering, one puts the plasmon peak inside a range that stops 15 nm short of it, and one silently equates two sampling intervals that the paper prints with different powers of ten. The most instructive correction is C41: the discourse said the standard uncertainties printed by a refinement are "the width of the valley, not its length", and that is wrong — the marginal variances contain the valley's projection on each axis; what reading the diagonal loses is the valley's direction, which is the correlation matrix. The audit's independent reasoning is right every time it is used.

Where this review narrows the audit, it is in five findings whose "website claim" is a paraphrase stronger than the sentence on the page (C18, C20, C23, C37, C42): the verdicts survive, the proposed wordings are still worth applying, but the reader of the audit should check the quoted claim against the page before concluding that the site said it. Three findings are only partly agreed (C13, C24, C36), for reasons given on their cards; two cannot be settled with the material in hand (C03, C40). None is rejected.

The audit also has gaps, listed under "The audit as a whole": it did not read the comparison table of the Cases page, which carries the same claims in a third place and one claim that C26 contradicts; it did not check the ISODISTORT half of the statement that mode amplitudes are written out as refinable parameters; and it applied the standard of a theorem to a page that announces itself as a first version to be argued over — rightly, since the project chose its three words to be used strictly, but the audit's "incorrect as written" should be read as "false in the strict vocabulary the site adopted", not as "the practitioner's point is wrong".

C07Agreed: apply

Homometry versus a linear null space

Audit said

Needs qualification — homometric structures are a discrete exact ambiguity, not a linear null space nor a continuous family; the audit's counterexample is \(F(x) = x^2\).

Website sentence

The discourse's definition: "Structural non-identifiability — an exact null space of the forward map … Patterson's homometric structures … are distinct arrangements with the same set of interatomic vectors and hence the same intensities."

Audit findingC07 in the audit
Assessment

Agreed. The definition box mixes a continuous notion (null space) with a discrete example (homometry). Since the project fixed its vocabulary deliberately, the fix belongs in the definition itself: an exact ambiguity is any pair of configurations with the same response; it may be a continuous family (a fibre of positive dimension) or a discrete set (homometry, the phase problem's enantiomorph pair); "null space" is the local, linear picture of the first.

Decision

Rewrite the definition with the two kinds named; keep Patterson as the discrete example. One change serves C07 and C17.

C12Agreed: apply

Primary-mode hierarchy and sparse priors

Audit said

C13Partly agreed

Symmetry modes do not necessarily diagonalize the data sensitivity

Audit said

Incorrect as written — the papers do not establish that symmetry modes are singular vectors of the instrument-weighted Jacobian; Perez-Mato's privileged eigenmodes are those of the energy Hessian; an orthogonal change of basis preserves the singular values.

Website sentence

The discourse, §4, point 2: "When two atoms move together by symmetry, a powder pattern sees their joint displacement and not the difference; in atomic coordinates that is a sloppy valley between two parameters, in mode coordinates it is one amplitude that is well determined and one that is small and may be fixed. The general principle: choose the coordinates in which the near-null space of the Jacobian is aligned with the axes."

Audit findingC13 in the audit
Checked against

Perez-Mato pp. 564–565, re-read today: the eigenmodes are "eigenmodes of the matrix of second derivatives of the free energy with respect to the atomic displacements" — the energy, as the audit says, not the measurement. Transtrum 010901-4: the Fisher matrix transforms as a metric under reparameterisation.

Assessment

The first part stands: the example sentence asserts that the symmetric combination is seen and the antisymmetric one is not, and nothing cited shows it; which combination a powder pattern determines depends on the structure factors, not on the symmetry alone. The audit's algebra is right: an orthogonal \(B\) leaves the singular values alone, so no basis change can shorten a valley; it can only name it. Not agreed is the paraphrase: the second sentence states the principle as an aim ("choose the coordinates in which…"), not as a property the papers establish, and the attribution line of §4 already marks the reading as this development's. The honest position, then: the alignment of the symmetry basis with the sensitivity basis is a hypothesis, plausible for symmetry-related pairs, and exactly what P2 is designed to test. The finding sharpens the programme rather than weakening it. A candidate source for the practitioners' experience that mode refinements converge with fewer correlations — Kerman et al. 2012, J. Appl. Cryst., on superstructure determination by symmetry-mode analysis — is noted from memory and not checked.

Decision

Rewrite point 2 as a hypothesis with its test (P2); keep the principle as an aim; cite Perez-Mato for the energy eigenmodes and say they are not the measurement's; add the candidate source to the Sources page as "not checked".

C16Agreed: apply

A small singular value needs a parameter scale

Audit said

Incorrect as written — the singular values of the noise-whitened Jacobian change with the units and scales of the parameters; "much smaller than one" means nothing until the parameters are scaled.

Website sentence

The discourse, §3: "a singular value of zero is a structural non-identifiability, a singular value much smaller than one is a practical one."

Audit findingC16 in the audit
Checked against

Transtrum 010901-4, re-read today: changing a rate constant from Hz to kHz scales its row and column of the Fisher matrix by 1000; dimensionless (log) parameters make the eigenvalues meaningful.

Assessment

Agreed, and it is a real omission: P1 asks programs to print this spectrum, and without a scale the spectrum is arbitrary. The natural scale is the one the project already talks about — the prior width of each parameter (a mode amplitude's plausible range, an occupancy's 0 to 1) — so the fix is to whiten the parameters as well as the data: \(J_s = \Gamma^{-1/2}\,\partial F/\partial\theta\,S\) with \(S\) the matrix of parameter scales.

Decision

Apply in §3 and in P1; "small" then means "small relative to one standard unit of prior width per unit of noise".

C17Agreed: apply

Local Jacobian rank is not a complete structural-identifiability test

Audit said

Incorrect as written — a zero singular value is local and first-order (\(x^3\) at 0); a discrete ambiguity has full rank (\(x^2\) at \(\pm 1\)); structural non-identifiability is not always a positive-dimensional fibre.

Website sentence

The discourse, §3 and §9: "a singular value of zero is a structural non-identifiability"; "Structural non-identifiability: a fibre of positive dimension."

Audit findingC17 in the audit

C18Agreed, with a narrower reading of the text

A likelihood region is not a posterior

Audit said

Website sentence

Audit findingC18 in the audit

C26Agreed: apply

Phase recovery needs analytic and geometry conditions

Audit said

Needs qualification — the log-reflectivity relation needs \(r \neq 0\) in the upper half-plane; it fails for sufficiently oblique incidence and for thin films; the relation is to \(\ln r\), not to \(r\).

Website sentence

The dielectric case: "the phase is fixed by the modulus over the whole axis, which recovers what the measurement did not record."

Audit findingC26 in the audit
Checked against

Kuzmenko 083108-1, Eqs. C36Partly agreed

Low-frequency Drude degeneracy is a limiting approximation

Audit said

Needs qualification — Eq. (4) keeps separate dependence on \(\omega_p\) and \(\tau\) at finite frequency; only in a reduced leading-order model do the data depend on the product alone.

Website sentence

The discourse's definition: "In the Drude model at low frequencies only the product \(\omega_p^2\tau\), the dc conductivity, is seen: a spectrum confined to that range cannot separate the two parameters at all." The Cases table: "At long wavelengths only \(\omega_p^2\tau\) (the dc conductivity) enters."

Audit findingC36 in the audit
Assessment

The audit's algebra is right: for \(\omega\tau \ll 1\), \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{II}} \approx \omega_p^2\tau/\omega\) carries the product and \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{I}} \approx 1 - \omega_p^2\tau^2\) carries a different combination, so the full expression is not a function of the product alone; what makes the reflectance of a good metal depend on \(\sigma_{\mathrm{dc}}\) alone in the Hagen–Rubens regime is that \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{II}}\) dominates. But the site files the example under the exact kind, and by its own vocabulary a limit is not exact. Agreed on the placement; not agreed on the paraphrase.

Decision

Keep the example, name the Hagen–Rubens limit, and move it to the practical kind: "in the limit, only the product is seen; at finite frequency the separate sensitivities exist and may be too weak to resolve."

A covariance diagonal is not a conditional tube cross-section

Audit said

Incorrect as written — in the local Gaussian picture the diagonal entries are marginal variances, \(\mathrm{Cov}_{ii} = \sum_k V_{ik}^2/\sigma_k^2\), and the small singular values contribute to them; reading the diagonal loses orientation and correlation, not the long directions.

Website sentence

Audit findingC41 in the audit
Assessment

Agreed, and this is the correction with the most consequence for the discourse's argument, because the two sentences are used to say what P1 should add. The algebra is right: a sloppy direction with a component along a parameter's axis makes that parameter's marginal variance large, so the printed e.s.d. does carry the valley's projection. The audit as a whole

What it does well, and what the project should keep doing:

  • It separates fidelity from truth. Each finding says whether the source supports the sentence, and apart from that whether the sentence is right; the two can come apart (C21: the numbers are right, the meaning drawn from them is not).
  • It states its access. Full text, abstract only, indirect, bibliographic: the kind of evidence behind every finding is on the page, and the verdicts on abstract-only sources (C07, C08, C17 in part) are about the website's wording, where an abstract is enough.
  • Its independent reasoning is right every time. The elementary counterexamples of C07, C13, C17, C28, C36 and C41 are correct and decisive; none of them needed a source.
  • It found the three factual errors about the one paper the project read in full (C33, C34, C35), which is the sharpest test a citation audit can pass: the auditor read the pages more carefully than the author did.

Where the audit should be read with care:

  • Five "website claims" are paraphrases stronger than the printed sentence (C18, C20, C23, C37, C42; and C13's and C24's in part). A future audit should quote the sentence verbatim and put the paraphrase after it, so that the reader can see the gap. The verdicts survive in all five cases; the paraphrases would not.
  • The standard applied is that of a theorem statement. Four "incorrect as written" verdicts (C13, C16, C27, C28) turn on the strict meaning of "null space", "singular value" and "not identifiable". The project chose to use its three words strictly and is bound by that choice, so the verdicts are accepted; but the practitioner's points behind the sentences (overlapping oscillators trade against each other; out-of-band weight is poorly constrained; an unscaled spectrum is still informative about order of magnitude) are true, and the corrections should keep them.
  • Some proposed wordings replace an argument with a hedge. "Assess", "validate", "test" are right as instructions to the programme and flat as sentences of a discourse. The corrections will be applied in the site's voice, with the content the audit asks for.

What the audit did not cover (gaps), each a small task:

  • G1 — the Cases comparison table (cases/index.qmd) was not audited. It repeats the claims of the three case pages in a third place and contains at least one the audit contradicts: "The phase of \(\tilde r\) from \(|\tilde r|\) alone" is listed under "Folded exactly", which C26 shows is false for the standard case. The table must be corrected together with the pages.
  • G2 — the ISODISTORT half of the mode-output sentence (that it writes amplitudes as refinable parameters for Rietveld programs) was not checked; C10 checked AMPLIMODES only.
  • G3 — the twelve theses of the brief are preserved as attributed ideas, rightly; but T07's own claim (that mode analysis "discards" coordinate values by symmetry) is the sentence C11 and C13 bear on, and the discussion with Iñigo should know that.
  • G4 — the discourse's attribution lines ("this development's", "Iñigo's") were not read as claims. They are the site's own provenance and are what makes C13's correction cheap: the sentence was already marked as a reading, not a result.

Decisions proposed

Nothing has been applied. The proposal is one revision of the discourse, the three case pages, the comparison table, the programme and the Sources page, logged as a single entry when Josu says so, with each change naming its finding. In four groups:

  • Facts, to apply as the audit words them: C33, C34, C35 (the Drude case); C25, C26, C27 (the dielectric case); C30 (a reference added); C21, C22, C32 (labels and scopes).
  • Vocabulary, to apply in one pass over §2, §3 and §9 of the discourse: C07 and C17 (exact ambiguities, continuous and discrete; the Jacobian as the local diagnostic of the continuous case); C16 (parameters scaled before the spectrum is read); C41 (the e.s.d.'s are projections; the correlation matrix is the direction); C36 (the Hagen–Rubens example moved to the practical kind); C28 (the oscillator example moved likewise).
  • Claims softened in the site's voice: C08, C11, C12, C14, C18, C20, C23, C37, C38, C39, C42; C13 and C24 as hypotheses with their tests (P2, P7).
  • Left as they are, with locators added: C02, C05, C06, C09, C10, C15, C19, C29, C31; C03 and C40 until the material is obtained.

And three rules for the project, from what the audit taught:

  • An audit quotes the sentence it judges, verbatim, before its paraphrase.
  • Two rankings are reported apart: a parameter's contribution to the structure and its identifiability from the data (C14).
  • A claim about the field ("not done", "the best results") is made only after a search, and says so (C24).

    What this is

    The working site of a line of thought: inverse problems whose theory is known but whose experiment collapses the configuration space, so that the parameters cannot be read back from the measurement without a tool brought from outside — a change of coordinates, a prior, another experiment, knowledge written into scripts and learned over at scale. The ideas are Iñigo González de Arrieta's; this is their development, with three cases, a programme, the sources and a dated log.
