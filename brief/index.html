The brief

The brief

The founding note of 14 October 2026, organised into twelve theses, then kept verbatim below. The ideas are Iñigo González de Arrieta's, shared with Josu; Josu's brief is to think on them and develop them. Ids never change; theses added later are appended with the log entry that adds them.

The problem

  • T01 Inverse problems in which the theory is known — the model that governs the response of the system to the probe — but the values of the parameters that enter it are poorly known.
  • T02 The experiment itself is underdetermined: too many parameters are involved for what the measurement can tell apart.
    Named as a second key concept, next to the poor knowledge of the parameter values.
  • T03 Knowing the theory is therefore not enough to infer the actual behaviour of the system, nor to say why the observed response is what it is.

The picture

  • T04 The experiment is a kind of projection. In that projection many parameters are collapsed: the configuration space of the system is collapsed onto the space of what is measured.
  • T05 Extracting the values of the parameters from the projection is not evident, not easy, and perhaps not possible at all — unless something else, an external tool, is brought in.

The known case

  • T06 Powder diffraction: the result of a Rietveld refinement of a crystal structure is compatible with many combinations of atomic coordinates, when the coordinates are the degrees of freedom used.
  • T07 Mode crystallography is the external tool there: it explores the configuration space in another set of independent coordinates, the amplitudes of the symmetry-adapted modes. After the analysis the structure is again described by atomic coordinates, but many of their possible values have been discarded by the symmetry the mode analysis searched with.

The proposal

  • T08 In these highly underdetermined inverse problems, bring in other tools: machine learning over thorough, massive data sets.
  • T09 Those data sets are to be treated massively and automatically by scripts, not analysed by hand one experiment at a time.
  • T10 The valuable user knowledge is not applied by hand to each data set; it is used to write the scripts that treat the data — the symmetry in diffraction is the example of super-user knowledge put in a script.

Scope and method

  • T11 The paper in the project (Daun et al. 2019, Drude parameters of liquid germanium nanoparticles from extinction measurements) is another example of the same situation; it is not to become the whole subject.
  • T12 Method: analyse the ideas, put them in a sorted, coherent discourse, think and rethink them; then elaborate, propose, innovate; then discuss. The work follows the protocol of the FOCUS–difes and FullProf (diff-ref-struct) projects: a website holding everything, a repository, a log on the site, and the sources used.

On this page

    What this is

    The working site of a line of thought: inverse problems whose theory is known but whose experiment collapses the configuration space, so that the parameters cannot be read back from the measurement without a tool brought from outside — a change of coordinates, a prior, another experiment, knowledge written into scripts and learned over at scale. The ideas are Iñigo González de Arrieta's; this is their development, with three cases, a programme, the sources and a dated log.
