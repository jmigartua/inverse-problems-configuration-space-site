The programme P1–P7 and the questions Q1–Q6 for Josu and Iñigo

Seven proposals, ordered from the cheapest to the largest, on the Programme page: P1 the diagnostic every fit should print (stiffness spectrum, sloppy directions in parameter names, credible region); P2 a toy that makes the thesis visible on a synthetic perovskite distortion, refined in coordinates and in mode amplitudes from many starting points; P3 the corpus — one structural family, patterns simulated under instrument models, the expert's analysis scripted (mode decomposition against a chosen parent, release strategy), the whole result stored, not one point; P4 learning over the corpus: identifiability maps, amortised posteriors over mode amplitudes, inadequacy flags; P5 the same scheme for infrared spectra with difes' recipes and libraries; P6 the Daun problem redone with a Mie surrogate and the design computation of which spectral band pins (\(\omega\)_p, \(\tau\)); P7 a perspective paper, after P2.

L0052026-10-14work

The first discourse: three kinds of indeterminacy, the projection made geometric, and the mode-crystallography move generalised

Written on the Discourse page, from the twelve theses. What it adds to the brief:

  • A distinction the brief folds into one word, "underdetermined": an exact null space of the forward map (structural non-identifiability: powder averaging, the phase problem, homometric structures); a near-null space at the resolution and noise of the instrument (practical non-identifiability, the sloppy valleys of Gutenkunst and Transtrum; Daun's Fig. 9a); and model inadequacy, where the truth is not on the model manifold at all (Rayleigh against Mie; the free-electron model against liquid germanium). The three need different external tools.
  • The projection written down: a forward map from a configuration space to a data space, its fibre over a measurement, the tube the noise makes of the fibre, and a refinement as one point of that tube chosen by the starting point and the strategy. The singular values of the weighted Jacobian as the local stiffness spectrum; its singular vectors as the coordinates the experiment actually sees.
  • Why symmetry modes work, stated so that it can be carried elsewhere: an orthogonal change of basis in which the physics' prior is sparse (a few primary amplitudes, the rest near zero) and in which the correlations the data cannot break become single coordinates. The parent structure is a hypothesis brought from outside; that is the irreducible expert input. The analogues named for the other two cases: Kramers–Kronig and sum rules for the dielectric function; other experiments and the model-error term for the extinction problem.
  • What machine learning can and cannot do here: it cannot recreate what the projection destroyed; it can return the whole fibre instead of a point (amortised inference), learn priors from corpora, replace expensive forward models, flag model inadequacy that recurs across data sets, and say which measurement would cut the fibre best. The output must be a distribution; a point estimate from an underdetermined problem is a confident guess.

L0032026-10-14finding

Daun et al. 2019 read: the ill-posedness is shown as a credible region, and the external tools are other experiments and a model-error term

The paper infers the Drude parameters (\(\omega\)_p, \(\tau\)) of liquid germanium nanoparticles from line-of-sight extinction spectra measured across an aerosol in a plasma reactor, with a halogen lamp (410–700 nm) and a laser-driven source (205–585 nm). Three forward models: Rayleigh, exact Mie, and a fourth-order approximation to Mie. The inference is Bayesian (MCMC, \(10^{6}\) samples), with a noise model fitted to the residuals of a smoothed spectrum and, for the approximate model, a model-error term after Kaipio and Somersalo. What matters for the thesis:

  • All candidate models reproduce the shape of the measured spectra, and the Drude parameters they return differ: ellipsometry 2.81 and 1.87; bulk density and resistivity 2.40 and 2.66; Rayleigh 2.85 and 4.5; Mie 2.04 and 1.97 (\(\omega\)_p in \(10^{16}\) rad/s, \(\tau\) in \(10^{-16}\) s; Table 3). The paper's own words: "the fact that different Drude parameters can produce similar extinction spectra highlights the ill-posedness of this problem".
  • Fig. 9 is the collapse made visible: with the halogen lamp and Rayleigh the 90 % credible region is a long curved valley in (\(\omega\)_p, \(\tau\)); with the approximate Mie model it is narrow; with the laser-driven source, which reaches the surface-plasmon resonance in the ultraviolet, it is narrower still. The information is in a part of the spectrum one source does not reach.
  • The external tools are other experiments (ellipsometry, bulk density and resistivity) and the explicit model-error term; and they disagree with the extinction-derived values in a statistically significant way. The conclusion is not a value but a diagnosis: the free-electron model is inadequate (perhaps three conduction electrons per atom, not four).
  • A simplified model (Rayleigh) that fits the data superficially gives a wrong picture; the credible region, not the fit, tells.

The brief

Josu writes down ideas that are Iñigo González de Arrieta's, shared with him, to think on them and develop them. The ideas: inverse problems whose theory is known but whose parameters are poorly known and too many for what the experiment can tell apart; the experiment as a projection that collapses the configuration space; the extraction of the parameters from the projection as something not evident, perhaps impossible, without an external tool; Rietveld refinement as the known case and mode crystallography as the external tool there; the proposal to bring in machine learning over massive data sets treated automatically by scripts into which the expert's knowledge has been written, instead of applied by hand to one data set at a time. The paper in the folder (Daun et al. 2019) is another example, not the subject.

Asked for: analyse, sort into a coherent discourse, think and rethink, elaborate, propose, innovate; then discuss. The protocol of the FOCUS–difes and FullProf (diff-ref-struct) projects: a website holding everything, a repository, the log on the site, the sources used.

The brief is kept verbatim and organised into twelve theses (T01–T12) on the Brief page.

    What this is

    The working site of a line of thought: inverse problems whose theory is known but whose experiment collapses the configuration space, so that the parameters cannot be read back from the measurement without a tool brought from outside — a change of coordinates, a prior, another experiment, knowledge written into scripts and learned over at scale. The ideas are Iñigo González de Arrieta's; this is their development, with three cases, a programme, the sources and a dated log.
